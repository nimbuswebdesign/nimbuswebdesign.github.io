How this is different

A site can look finished and still have problems beneath the surface. We check whether its pages can be reached, whether each page clearly describes its purpose, and whether the site points search engines consistently to the intended addresses.

Every site built here ships with an automated audit wired into the build. It checks titles, descriptions, canonicals, heading structure, image alt text, structured data, sitemap coverage in both directions, orphaned pages and broken internal links. If any of it fails, the build fails and the site does not deploy. That is the difference between a site that was checked once and a site that stays correct.

A local page should help someone understand the work you do in that area. Repeating the same page with different town names adds little for the visitor and can create search-quality problems. Read our guide to useful local pages.

Is my website actually findable?

Start with a site:yourdomain.com search, then confirm individual pages in Search Console. Search results are a useful spot check, but they are not a complete inventory of indexed pages.

Why your Google Business Profile beats your website

Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Accurate business details and genuine reviews help customers assess you; Google does not publish fixed percentage weights or guarantee a position.

Why city pages usually backfire

A city page should answer a real local need with useful, distinct information. Near-duplicate pages can compete for the same intent, and pages made mainly to funnel similar searches can violate Google's doorway-abuse policy.
