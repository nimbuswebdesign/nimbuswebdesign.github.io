What the review actually covers

The review combines checks of your website with a look at how your business appears in search. It covers:

  • Whether every page has its own title and description, or they share one
  • Whether your canonical tags point at the page actually being served
  • Whether your sitemap and your real pages agree, in both directions
  • Pages nothing links to, and internal links that do not resolve
  • Whether your words are in the HTML or drawn later by scripts
  • Missing image alt text, and images that shift the layout while loading
  • Errors in structured data that could make it ineligible for search features
  • Visible gaps in your Google Business Profile, such as missing services or business details

You get back a written list in plain language, ordered by what would move the needle most, with the things you can fix yourself marked as such. There is no obligation attached to it, and if the honest conclusion is that your site is fine and the problem is your profile or your reviews, that is what it will say.

What to send

Your domain is enough to start. If you have more context, these help: what you'd like the site to bring in, anything you already know that customers ask you, and whether you have access to your own domain registrar — that last one matters more than people expect.

If you're comparing quotes from other people right now, send those too. A second opinion on what you've been offered is a reasonable thing to want and it doesn't obligate you to anything. These are the questions worth asking before you sign with anyone, including us.

Calls are usually easier than email for a first conversation. Fifteen minutes is normally enough to work out whether there's anything worth doing.
