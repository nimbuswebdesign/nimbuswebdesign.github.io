How the work goes

  1. Measure before changing anything

    Record the dates, queries, landing pages and filters behind the baseline. Check technical issues against the site and use Search Console for Google's indexing and performance reports. A test of the website does not establish where it ranks.

  2. Fix what is structurally broken first

    Check access to important pages, broken links, indexing directives and preferred URLs. Fix confirmed problems before adding content. A crawlable page is eligible to be considered, but technical corrections alone do not establish relevance or guarantee indexing.

  3. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate

    Review the category, services, hours, contact details and photos against what the business actually offers. Genuine reviews help customers assess you. Listing a service area does not move your business closer to a searcher or guarantee visibility throughout that area.

  4. Build content that answers real questions

    Explain the service, who it suits, what is included, costs where known and what happens next. Add real examples when available. Improve a useful existing page before creating overlapping pages for small variations of the same search.

  5. Earn links locally, not from a directory list

    Look for relevant opportunities through real projects, professional relationships, associations and local coverage. Assess whether a listing helps people find or evaluate the business. Buying links to manipulate ranking is not part of the work; there is no promised link count or ranking value.

  6. Report what actually happened

    Report search clicks and impressions with their date range and filters. Record location and device when sampling rankings. Keep phone-button clicks separate from answered calls, and compare received inquiries and won work when the business can confirm them.

What people usually arrive with

“We pay someone monthly and nothing changes”

Ask what specifically was done last month and whether you can verify it. Legitimate SEO produces artifacts — pages, fixes, profile changes, links — that you can point at. If the answer is a report of rankings with no work attached to it, there may be no work attached to it.

“We were told we need a hundred city pages”

A location page should offer distinct, useful information for customers there. Replacing only the town name adds little value, and pages created mainly to funnel similar searches can violate Google's doorway-abuse policy. We assess the purpose and evidence for each page before building it.

“We rank fine at the office and badly everywhere else”

That's proximity, and it's the one local ranking factor nobody can optimize. What you can do is build the other two — relevance and prominence — until they carry you further from your own front door. It's also why ranking claims should always state where the search was run.

“Someone offered guaranteed number one rankings”

Nobody can guarantee that, because nobody controls the ranking. Anyone offering it is either describing a search term so specific that nobody types it, or is about to do something that will cost you later.

Common questions

How long before SEO does anything?

Timing varies. Google may need to recrawl changed pages, and the effect can take weeks or months to assess. We report the work immediately and compare search data over suitable periods as it becomes available. We do not promise a ranking improvement by a particular date.

Is SEO still worth it now that AI answers questions directly?

Useful content and accessible pages still matter. We focus on accurate answers, real business evidence and search eligibility, then examine the reporting available for each search feature. No special format or markup guarantees selection in an AI answer.

Do you do the Google Business Profile too?

Yes. With your account access, profile accuracy can be the agreed monthly priority. We review relevant categories, services, hours and photos. Work stays within the plan's four-hour total allowance, including care and reporting; larger work is quoted separately.

What can you not promise?

A specific position, a timeline for one, or that a competitor won't outrank you. Rankings depend on competitors, proximity, reviews and links — most of which sit outside anything a site can control. What can be promised is that the work is real, visible, and checkable.

Is my website actually findable?
